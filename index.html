💾 spaceguard — Never Run Out of Disk Space Again

spaceguard sits quietly in your menu bar, keeps an eye on how much room is left on your Mac, and clears out the clutter that quietly piles up — old developer caches, leftover iPhone simulator versions and abandoned coding-agent worktrees from Claude Code, Codex and Cursor.

🧭 What spaceguard Does

Your Mac has a fixed amount of storage. Every day you use it, small things add up: temporary files from building software, downloaded simulator versions for iPhone and iPad testing, and leftover copies of project folders created automatically by AI coding assistants. Individually these are tiny. Together they can eat tens of gigabytes — and you usually find out only when your Mac warns you that the startup disk is almost full.

spaceguard changes that. It lives in your menu bar at the top of the screen, so it is always running but never in the way. It checks your free space on a regular basis and shows the number at a glance. When storage drops below the level you choose, it identifies the safe-to-delete items and clears them out for you.

No terminal windows. No commands to memorise. No hunting through hidden folders. One click in the menu bar, and the space comes back.

✨ Key Features

📊 Live free-space monitoring

A small readout in the menu bar shows how much room is left at all times. Open the panel for a simple breakdown of what is using the most space.

🧽 Automatic cleaning of stale dev caches

Build systems and package managers keep copies of downloaded and compiled files to speed things up. Many of those copies are never used again. spaceguard finds the ones that have gone stale and removes them, freeing space without touching your projects or your work.

📱 Old simulator runtimes

Testing an iPhone or iPad app stores a full operating-system image for each simulator version you install. Old versions can sit there for years taking up several gigabytes each. spaceguard spots the ones that are out of date or unused and offers to remove them.

🤖 Coding-agent worktrees

AI coding tools such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor often create temporary working copies of your code so they can experiment without disturbing your main folder. When a session ends, those copies can be left behind. spaceguard identifies the abandoned ones and cleans them up, keeping the ones that are still active.

🎚️ You stay in control

Choose how much free space you want to keep protected. Choose whether spaceguard cleans automatically or simply tells you when it is time. Every action is reported in plain language, so you always know what changed.

🍃 Light on resources

spaceguard is a native Mac application built with Swift and SwiftUI. It uses almost no memory and does not slow your Mac down while it waits in the background.

🚀 Getting Started

Step 2 — Choose the newest version

The page lists every version of spaceguard ever published, newest at the top. Pick the entry marked as the latest release and open it. You will see the files that belong to that version listed underneath.

Step 3 — Open the app

Save the file to your computer, then open it from your Downloads folder. spaceguard will start and place its icon in the menu bar at the top of your screen. Click that icon to see your free space and begin cleaning.

💡 If your Mac shows a message the first time you open a new app, that is normal for software downloaded from the internet. Confirm that you want to open it, and spaceguard will start.

🧹 What Gets Cleaned

spaceguard is deliberately narrow in what it touches. It only looks after the categories below — the ones that grow without limits and the ones you are least likely to miss.

  • Stale developer caches — temporary build files, downloaded packages and compiled artefacts that have not been used recently.
  • Outdated simulator runtimes — old iPhone and iPad system images that are no longer needed for testing.
  • Abandoned coding-agent worktrees — temporary project copies left behind by Claude Code, Codex and Cursor after a session ends.
  • Loose temporary files — leftover scraps created during normal everyday use.

Your documents, photos, applications, music and personal files are never part of the cleanup. spaceguard does not touch them.

🛡️ Is It Safe?

Yes. Every item spaceguard removes is a duplicate, a cache or an abandoned leftover — something that can be regenerated automatically the next time it is needed. Nothing is deleted permanently: items you clean are moved to the Mac Trash, so if something ever looks wrong you can put it straight back.

You also decide how much space should always stay free. If a cleanup would take you below that limit, spaceguard stops rather than clearing more.

Before anything is removed, you can review the list. Nothing happens silently behind your back.

💡 Tips for Best Results

  • Leave spaceguard running in the menu bar so it can spot growth early, before it becomes a problem.
  • Run a cleanup after finishing a big project or after a long session with an AI coding assistant — that is when the most leftovers pile up.
  • Set your protected free-space limit generously. Keeping a comfortable buffer gives your Mac room to breathe and stay fast.
  • Check the panel occasionally. Seeing where your space goes helps you decide what to keep and what to let go.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know anything about programming?

No. spaceguard was made for people who simply want their computer to keep working without storage warnings. Everything is done through the menu bar with ordinary clicks.

Will it delete my files or projects?

No. It only clears temporary and abandoned items, and it moves them to the Trash rather than erasing them outright. Your personal files and working projects stay exactly where they are.

Does it slow my computer down?

No. spaceguard rests quietly in the menu bar and only becomes active when it checks your free space or runs a cleanup.

How often should I clean?

As often as you like. Many people let spaceguard handle it automatically and only glance at the menu bar number. Others prefer to press the button themselves once a month.

Where do I get the newest version?

Always from the releases page: https://github.com/Konnuyu/spaceguard/releases. Visit this link to download the application.

🆘 Getting Help

If something does not look right, open an issue on the repository and describe what you saw. Include the version of spaceguard you are using and what your menu bar showed at the time — that is usually enough to work out what happened.

Feature ideas are welcome too. If there is a type of clutter spaceguard should learn to clean, say so.
